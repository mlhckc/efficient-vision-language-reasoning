\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}

\section{Overall conclusion}
\label{sec:ch9-overall}

This dissertation examined whether lightweight systems over frozen
representations can support efficient closed-set visual question answering,
and where the limits of that formulation lie. Under the governed development
protocol, compact heads produced useful GQA accuracy while retaining low
measured warm-serial latency. Interaction and representation choices affected
the observed results, whereas additional downstream capacity did not provide a
single explanation for the strongest configurations.

Small language models can contribute to lightweight vision--language
reasoning, but they were not required for the strongest measured
accuracy--latency trade-offs in this study. Their pretraining benefit was
interface-specific. These conclusions rest on development evidence; the
held-out clean-test evaluation remains pending.

\section{Answers to the research questions}
\label{sec:ch9-answers}

\textbf{RQ1} (Section~\ref{sec:ch1-rqs}) asked how effectively lightweight heads over frozen global image
and question embeddings answer closed-set GQA, and how interaction, encoder
choice and answer-set size affect development accuracy. The heads are viable
on the governed task. The question-only result shows strong predictive
information in the question/answer distribution, while the image
interventions show material dependence on the correct image rather than
grounding or reasoning. Standard fusion improves over standard concatenation
at the lower training scale. Increasing concatenation capacity accounts for
part of that original gap, while the remaining capacity-matched difference is
not resolved clearly. Encoder choice matters in the tested configurations,
but that comparison is representation-confounded. Expanding the answer set
improves coverage while defining a different in-vocabulary task
(Section~\ref{sec:ch4-summary}).

\textbf{RQ2} asked whether the 32-query latent reasoner improves on global
heads as training data scales, including on questions with four or more
program steps. Its development accuracy rises across the tested scales. The
same-seed gaps over fusion are positive in every seed at 100k and 250k, but
not consistently at 40k. This remains a configuration-level comparison
because architecture, token access and capacity co-vary. The pooled
four-or-more-step deficit persists, and the paired reasoner-minus-fusion
deficit differences remain unresolved. Higher overall accuracy therefore does
not establish that compositional difficulty has been removed
(Section~\ref{sec:ch5-summary}). The observed deficit was not eliminated by
the implemented changes in architecture, token access, downstream capacity or
training scale, but the experiments do not localise its cause.

\textbf{RQ3} asked whether frozen small-language-model pretraining helps on
the question or answer side, and what efficiency implications the
trainable-parameter counts and valid warm-serial measurements support.
On the question side, pretrained SmolLM2 exceeds its architecture-matched
random control at both tested scales, yet remains below the CLIP-token
reference under the tested configurations. Pretraining therefore helps within
that SLM interface without establishing that the SLM representation is
generally preferable. On the answer side, the 135M contrast is negative
directional at 40k and uncertain or mixed at 250k; both 360M contrasts and the
second-order scale interaction are uncertain or mixed. No reliable positive
answer-side pretraining advantage was detected, while the unresolved results
establish neither equivalence nor absence. The conclusion is
interface-specific rather than universal under the implemented interfaces and
three separately run experiments (Section~\ref{sec:ch6-summary}).

The efficiency result reinforces that distinction. The question-side SLM
interface has higher governed warm-serial latency than the global heads and
latent reasoner. The canonical E7b accuracy--latency frontier contains the
concatenation, fusion and top-1000 product systems; it contains no SLM system.
Trainable-parameter counts provide capacity context rather than a latency
proxy. E9 remains contextual with unresolved head pairing, and comparable R1
and E10 answer-side latency was not measured
(Section~\ref{sec:ch7-summary}).

\section{Contributions and limitations}
\label{sec:ch9-contributions}

This dissertation contributes a controlled development study of compact VQA
heads over frozen representations, including interaction and capacity
controls. It contributes an evaluation of a 32-query latent reasoner across
training scales with token-input and compositional-profile diagnostics. It
also contributes matched pretrained-versus-random SLM controls on both sides
of the reasoner, including the 360M answer-side extension, and a governed
end-to-end warm-serial evaluation with a canonical common-denominator
accuracy--latency frontier. The accompanying record that distinguishes
canonical, superseded and withdrawn evidence is a supporting reproducibility
contribution rather than a scientific result.

The conclusions remain bounded to development evidence from one closed-set GQA
setting. Supported top-100 and top-1000 results are different tasks;
cross-family comparisons are configuration-level; only a limited set of SLM
families, sizes and interfaces was tested; and efficiency claims cover only
the retained measured quantities on the governed nodes. Section~\ref{sec:ch8-limitations}
gives the full limitations.

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:ch9-future}

Each next step follows from one of those limits. If authorised, the next
validation step is to freeze the final governed system selection and perform a
blinded held-out clean-test evaluation. Capacity- and interface-matched
controls could separate representation choice from downstream architecture in
the current configuration-level comparisons. Broader small-language-model
families, placements and lightweight adaptation could test whether the
answer-side pattern reflects interface compatibility or model scale. A more
prior-controlled compositional analysis could test whether the persistent
profile survives a stricter proxy for reasoning difficulty. Finally, valid
answer-side latency and corrected-boundary memory measurements under the
existing protocol could complete the current efficiency evidence. None of
these tests presumes a favourable outcome.

\section{Final statement}
\label{sec:ch9-final}

Under the implemented development protocol, the evidence supports an
interface-specific contribution from small language models, but the strongest
measured accuracy--latency frontier does not require one. Compact
frozen-representation systems remain a viable basis for this closed-set task,
while the persistent compositional profile and interface-dependent pretraining
results define important limits. Held-out validation remains pending, so the
final claim is deliberately bounded to the development evidence reported here.
