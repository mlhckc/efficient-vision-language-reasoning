\chapter{Methodology: Data, Evaluation Protocol and Architectures}
\section{Methodological framing and design principles}
\label{sec:ch3-framing}
This chapter defines the methodology used to study closed-set visual question answering on GQA \citep{hudson2019gqa}. The central problem is whether lightweight trainable heads can make effective use of frozen multimodal representations, while keeping development-set accuracy and measured efficiency, understood here as warm-serial latency and trainable parameter count, in view. Within the global pathway, fixed representations come from the separate image and text towers of a frozen CLIP checkpoint \citep{radford2021clip}.
This broader use of frozen pretrained components is established in vision--language research \citep{tsimpoukelli2021frozen}, but here it is treated as a controlled design choice rather than a claim about any one architecture.

Four principles govern the experiments. First, representation encoders remain frozen, so comparisons concern the trainable interface or head. Second, matched controls are used wherever an architectural or pretraining choice is isolated. Third, models are replicated across predefined seeds rather than interpreted from a single run. Finally, development evidence is kept separate from future confirmatory evaluation: it supports model development and analysis, but not a held-out generalisation claim.

The formal research questions are to be stated in Chapter 1.

As an organising map, RQ1 concerns global heads and representation or answer-space choices; RQ2 concerns token-level reasoning under data scaling; and RQ3 concerns frozen small-language-model interfaces and their bounded efficiency evidence. Chapters 4--6 address these questions in turn, while Chapter 7 reports the efficiency analysis.

\section{Task definition: closed-set VQA on GQA}
\label{sec:ch3-task}
The experimental task uses the balanced release of GQA, a benchmark designed around questions grounded in image content and compositional scene structure \citep{hudson2019gqa}. Each example supplies an image and a natural-language question, and the system maps that pair to one answer from a fixed vocabulary. This is the primary task throughout the dissertation: discriminative closed-set VQA, rather than unrestricted language generation. Closed-vocabulary classification has a long-standing role in VQA evaluation \citep{antol2015vqa,jabri2016revisiting}; in this project, the answer vocabulary is constructed from the training pool only, so development images cannot determine its support.

Two later experiment families use bounded generation only as an evaluation readout. In the answer-side language-model family (E8B, described in Section 3.7), the R2 readout decodes greedily under a prefix trie restricted to the closed answer vocabulary, while the R3 readout removes that restriction but stays greedy and stops at a fixed token cap. The compact vision--language models used for context (E9, defined in Section 3.7) are likewise evaluated with a pinned greedy readout. These exceptions test how an already defined representation or readout produces an answer; they do not extend the task to open-ended generation. Sampling, temperature search and prompt search are excluded.

Keeping this distinction explicit prevents a change in output mechanism from being mistaken for a change in the scientific task. The same boundary applies to every classifier and readout family: the image--question input is fixed, answer production is bounded, and every trainable component is fitted to a fixed answer set defined for that experiment. The training objective differs by family: the classifier heads and the language-model-free readout use a cross-entropy loss over the closed answer vocabulary, whereas the answer-side language-model readouts are trained with a teacher-forced token-level loss on the answer tokens (each answer token is predicted from the preceding gold tokens), with decoding bounded at evaluation as described above. The scoring rules and evaluation denominators are defined in Section 3.5.

\section{Answer-space construction: top-100 and top-1000}
\label{sec:ch3-answer-space}
The main answer space contains the 100 most frequent raw answers in the training pool. Answers are ordered by decreasing count, with alphabetical order used to break ties. Development examples do not contribute to this construction. A second answer space extends the same rule to the 1,000 most frequent training-pool answers. Its first 100 entries are identical to the smaller vocabulary, including their indices, so the extension does not silently change the meaning of an existing class.

These choices define which development questions can be scored within each closed vocabulary. The top-100 vocabulary covers 7,714 of the 10,004 raw development rows, giving a coverage of 0.7711. The top-1000 extension covers 9,823 rows, or 0.9819. These figures describe answer-space coverage rather than model performance. They show the evaluation support for each experiment, not how accurately any system answers the retained questions.

Pinned protocol artefacts give each experiment its answer-to-index mapping. The shared prefix extends rather than revises the top-100 labels, keeping answer-space design separate from representation and architecture choices. Section~\ref{sec:ch3-scoring} defines how the vocabularies lead to different evaluation denominators.

\section{Scoring metrics and evaluation denominators}
\label{sec:ch3-scoring}
The primary strict metric is raw exact match: a prediction is correct only when its answer string exactly matches the single canonical gold answer. The project also uses one pinned normalised exact-match metric. It applies a fixed, single-gold translation of the standard VQA answer normalisation before making the same exact comparison. This retains the normalisation lineage of VQA evaluation \citep{antol2015vqa}, but it is not the official VQA soft or consensus accuracy, which averages agreement over several human answers.

Within the top-100 vocabulary, normalisation changes none of the answer strings and creates no collisions. Raw and normalised correctness therefore coincide for these classifiers. This is a property of the pinned answer set, not a general claim about normalised scoring. Semantic similarity, synonym matching, embedding-based scoring and language-model judgement are excluded from the primary metrics.

Table~\ref{tab:ch3-answer-spaces} separates the three reporting denominators: 7,714 top-100 rows, 9,823 top-1000 rows and all 10,004 raw development rows. They are never mixed silently. Across answer spaces, coverage-adjusted raw-distribution accuracy is the in-vocabulary accuracy multiplied by coverage. Project scores are not compared with published GQA scores \citep{hudson2019gqa}, because the answer spaces, scoring rule and denominators differ.

\section{Frozen representations and feature extraction}
\label{sec:ch3-representations}
The global pathway uses one frozen OpenCLIP ViT-B-32 checkpoint, \texttt{laion2b\_s34b\_b79k}. Its separate image and text towers \citep{ilharco2021openclip} produce L2-normalised 512-dimensional global vectors. The towers belong to one jointly pretrained checkpoint, so their outputs occupy an aligned pretrained space; this does not mean that the same encoder processes both modalities.
Freezing the representation model keeps training focused on the lightweight interface, following the broader frozen-model setting described by \citet{tsimpoukelli2021frozen}.

\begingroup
\setlength{\emergencystretch}{2em}
The SigLIP encoder comparison (E2) replaces only this global pathway with the frozen \texttt{ViT-B-16-}\allowbreak\texttt{SigLIP} \citep{zhai2023siglip} checkpoint using \texttt{webli} weights. It supplies 768-dimensional inputs to the same head recipe. The token-level stores used by the reasoner remain CLIP features, so this encoder swap is not carried into the token pathway.\par
\endgroup

The visual token store contains 50 vectors per image: one CLS token and 49 patch tokens, all with width 512. Question tokens are stored at their packed sequence lengths with the same width. These CLIP stores provide a jointly pretrained token space for the latent reasoner. The question-side small-language-model interface instead uses frozen SmolLM2-135M hidden states \citep{benallal2025smollm2} from a pinned model revision recorded in the provenance record. A trainable \texttt{Linear(576, 512)} layer connects those states to the reasoner. This common width is a learned interface and is not evidence that CLIP and SmolLM2 naturally share a pretrained semantic space. The 360M question-side arms were authorised but not run.

For the answer-side E8B family, canonical evaluation is performed in FP32. This precision rule applies only to E8B evaluation; it does not redefine training precision elsewhere.

\subsection{Global-embedding heads}
Global heads share an \texttt{MLPHead}, normally at hidden width 512. Its layers are linear, ReLU, dropout and linear. The \texttt{question\_only} and \texttt{image\_only} controls use one vector. \texttt{concat} uses $[i;q]$; \texttt{fusion} uses the two globals, their product and absolute difference in the order $[i;q;i\odot q;|i-q|]$.

Two capacity-matched controls change only the hidden width: \texttt{concat\_wide} (979) matches \texttt{fusion}'s parameter count, and \texttt{fusion\_narrow} (268) matches \texttt{concat}'s. Product-only and difference-only controls use width 351 (\texttt{product\_576k}, \texttt{difference\_576k}) or the natural width 512 (the separately executed \texttt{product\_natural}, \texttt{difference\_natural}). The \texttt{direct\_linear} reference is \texttt{Linear(1024, 100)}.

The SigLIP encoder comparison (E2) applies the same head family to 768-dimensional inputs and is not parameter-matched to the 512-dimensional heads. The top-1000 answer-space experiment (E3) changes the output to 1,000 classes. The capacity-matching experiment (v2\_03) varies capacity with the features fixed; the interaction ablation (v2\_04) varies the features at controlled capacity; E2 varies the frozen representation with the head recipe fixed.

\subsection{Token-level latent reasoner}
The reasoner uses 32 learned latent queries \citep{jaegle2021perceiver}. Four pre-layer-normalised blocks apply question cross-attention, image cross-attention, latent self-attention and a fourfold GELU feed-forward network, in that order. Cross-attention reads an input sequence; self-attention connects the latents. Attention has 8 heads at width 512. Because the token stores already lie in the 512-dimensional joint space, no separate learned input projection is applied before the attention blocks.

The final latents are mean-pooled and layer-normalised. The readout is \texttt{Linear(512,100)}. The reasoner has 21,099,620 trainable parameters. Figure~\ref{fig:ch3-reasoner} shows this order and the frozen inputs. Two references bracket the reasoner: a concat head over mean-pooled patch tokens, and the direct linear classifier.

\subsection{Question-side small-language-model interface}
The question-side interface supplies three inputs to one trainable reasoner and classifier. A0p applies \texttt{Linear(512,\allowbreak 512)} to frozen CLIP tokens. A1/A1r apply \texttt{Linear(576,\allowbreak 512)} to frozen pretrained/matched-random SmolLM2-135M states. The encoders stay frozen; a learned common width does not create a shared pretrained space.

\subsection{Answer-side small-language-model interface}
The answer-side path projects 32 latents into the frozen model's embedding width. B1 is an attention-pool classifier. B2/B3 are matched-random/pretrained 135M readouts; B4r/B4 are the 360M pair. Within each size the pretrained and random partners have identical trainable-parameter counts, because they share one architecture. R1 scores every candidate answer in the closed vocabulary by its likelihood under the frozen language model and selects the highest, while R2 and R3 are the trie-constrained greedy and bounded free-greedy readouts from Section~\ref{sec:ch3-task}. Frozen SmolVLM-256M-Instruct and SmolVLM-500M-Instruct \citep{marafioti2025smolvlm} are contextual only. They have zero trainable parameters and are not matched with the trained systems. The pinned Instruct checkpoints do not define a base-versus-Instruct contrast.

\subsection{Matched random controls}
A matched-random control keeps its partner's architecture and tokenizer. Pinned-seed random weights are frozen and not tuned separately. \section{Training, checkpoints and model selection}
\label{sec:ch3-training}
The controlled global-head studies (V2) used AdamW with learning rate
$10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch size 256 and dropout 0.3. They ran
for a fixed 30 epochs on \texttt{train\_40k} with seeds 0, 1, 2, 3 and 42.
A later V2 scaling study reused this recipe at 100k and 250k; the V2 row in
Table~\ref{tab:ch3-protocol} therefore describes only the controlled studies.

The latent reasoner (V3), the question-side interface and the language-model-free
B1 control shared the reasoner recipe. It used AdamW at $3\times10^{-4}$, with weight decay
$10^{-2}$ excluding biases and LayerNorm terms, batch size 128, gradient
clipping at 1.0 and a cosine schedule without warm-up. Training kept fp32
master weights with bf16 autocasting; evaluation used fp32. V3 covered 40k,
100k and 250k, while the interface families used 40k and 250k. All used seeds
0, 1 and 2, a 100-epoch budget and early-stopping patience of 10.

The answer-side language-model systems instead used learning rate
$3\times10^{-4}$, no warm-up, dropout 0.1 and exactly 22 epochs without
early stopping. Training used bf16 autocasting, with canonical fp32 evaluation
from the trainable trunk onwards. The 360M systems had a fixed evaluation
cadence, no overfit gate and no resumed runs. Section~3.2 defines
cross-entropy for classifiers and B1, and teacher-forced token loss for the
answer-side systems.

Table~\ref{tab:ch3-protocol} separates training from selection. V2, V3, the
question-side interface and B1 kept the checkpoint with the highest
development accuracy, using the earliest epoch on a tie. The answer-side
systems used the fixed epoch-22 checkpoint. Performance-based checkpoint
selection used the development split only. The answer-side systems did not
select a checkpoint by development accuracy; their epoch-22 checkpoint was
fixed by protocol. Every recipe was either fixed in advance or, for the
answer-side systems, retained as the pre-existing default without reference to
any observed result. Selection-matched contrasts support controlled comparisons
within the design.
B1 comparisons with B2 or B3 mix selection classes and retain that
methodological caveat.

\section{Statistical methodology}
\label{sec:ch3-statistics}
All seed sets were fixed in advance: five for V2 and three for the later
paired families. The mean summarises the trained seeds, while the sample
standard deviation (\texttt{ddof=1}) describes variation between training
runs. It is not an interval for evaluation-sample uncertainty.

Evaluation-sample uncertainty used an image-clustered bootstrap \citep{efron1993bootstrap,field2007clusterbootstrap}.
Images are resampled because questions referring to one image are not
independent rows. Each of 2,000 draws sampled represented development image
identifiers with replacement using \texttt{default\_rng(0)}, carrying all
of an image's questions together. Both sides of a paired comparison used the
same sampled images. Seed results were averaged within each draw, and the 2.5
and 97.5 percentiles formed the interval. It measures sampling uncertainty
conditional on the fixed trained seeds, so it is kept separate from seed
standard deviation.

For three-seed paired E8A, E8B and E10 comparisons, positive directional
evidence requires a mean difference above zero, an interval lower bound above
zero and at least two of three seed differences above zero. Negative evidence
uses the mirrored conditions; otherwise the category is \emph{uncertain or
mixed evidence}. This rule is not extended to another seed structure without
separate canonical authority. An interval containing zero establishes neither
equivalence nor absence of an effect. Intervals are nominal and uncorrected
for multiple comparisons.

The primary compositional quantity is a pooled deficit for questions with four
or more reasoning steps. Development questions are grouped into four step
buckets, and each bucket's lift is computed under a fixed per-bucket question
prior. The combined lift for four or more steps pools the four-step and
five-or-more-step buckets, weighted by those fixed priors. The pooled deficit
is the unweighted mean of the four bucket lifts minus that combined lift.
The definition is recorded without an outcome value. V2 scaling has no valid
reconstructed image-clustered interval, so reporting is limited to canonical
per-seed aggregates and seed variability.

\section{Visual-reliance intervention design}
\label{sec:ch3-reliance}
The initial visual-reliance protocol compared normal, shuffled and zeroed image
inputs for each model and seed. The shuffled condition permuted image
embeddings across development rows with recorded seed 42, while the zeroed
condition replaced them with zeros. Question embeddings were unchanged, and
the question-only head acted as an invariance control. For the fusion head,
zeroing changes its input to $[0;q;0;q]$.

The later E8 programme superseded row-level shuffling with an image-level
derangement. A permutation of sorted unique image identifiers is sampled until
no image remains paired with itself. One deterministic partner is then reused
for every row belonging to that image. This removes the 11 self-pairs, or
0.14 per cent, left by the earlier row-level method. The compact-VLM context
uses the same partner mapping, although its preprocessing means that the
feature-space intervention is not identical. These interventions measure
dependence on the correct image. They do not establish causal, compositional
or human-like visual reasoning.

\section{Efficiency measurement protocol}
\label{sec:ch3-efficiency}
Efficiency used deliberately separate protocols. The component study,
E7a, remains valid for isolated operations. Its additive end-to-end sums were
withdrawn because separately timed components did not define a complete
pipeline; no replacement or estimate was made.

The authoritative end-to-end study, E7b, used batch size 1 and one unbroken
serial pass from image-file reading to answer decoding. Three pass-major runs
followed a pinned randomised order, with a fresh subprocess for every system
and pass. After 50 warm-up queries, 300 queries were timed with device
synchronisation around each call. The headline measure was the warm-query
median. The node was otter155 with an RTX 4000 Ada GPU.

Two field groups were withdrawn for separate reasons. Peak allocated and
reserved memory had a measurement-boundary defect. Cold first-query time was
superseded after gradient-mode standardisation. Neither has a replacement or
estimate.

The compact-VLM context, E9, reused the frozen harness on otter159, but remains
node-specific contextual evidence. A bridge check failed its pre-registered
10 per cent tolerance. This protocol-validation fact means that otter155 and
otter159 timings are not merged or adjusted. Cached-feature measurements are
labelled partial-pipeline timings. Trainable parameter count describes
capacity, not latency. Claims are limited to measured warm-serial latency under
the stated protocol, trainable parameters and canonically reported model size.
Energy, power, carbon and monetary cost were not measured.

\section{Reproducibility, provenance and clean-test governance}
\label{sec:ch3-governance}
Reproducibility uses tracked mechanisms. \texttt{utils.set\_seed} fixes
Python, NumPy and PyTorch generators and the Python hash seed, enables
deterministic algorithms, and seeds data-loader generators and workers. Run
metadata records the commit, seed, library versions and device. A dependency
lock records the environment, while hashes pin artefacts and provenance.
Corrections use immutable amendment chains and supersession overlays instead
of rewriting closed records. Table conversion is deterministic and verified.
Evidence follows canonical repair and supersession authority, closure evidence,
canonical VE evidence, experiment evidence, current reports and finally
historical artefacts for provenance only. Local coordination packets are not
scientific evidence.

These mechanisms have limits. The environment is node-local and uses one GPU;
the historical E7b node is not the current node. Determinism must be re-imposed
after every reseeding point. The lock does not prove portability across
hardware or software stacks. No energy or power measurement exists.

Development evidence supports model development and diagnostics. Confirmatory
evidence requires a frozen model list and protocol followed by one clean
held-out evaluation.
