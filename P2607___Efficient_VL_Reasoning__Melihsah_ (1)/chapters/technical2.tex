\chapter{RQ2 — Latent Reasoning and Compositional Difficulty}

\section{Scope and comparison boundary}
\label{sec:ch5-scope}
RQ2 examines whether the token-level latent reasoner improves on the global
heads and how its accuracy changes across questions of different program
lengths. All results in this chapter use the top-100 development view: 7,714
questions over 768 represented images. They therefore describe development
evidence only and make no held-out claim. The reasoner architecture and its
32 learned queries are defined in Section~\ref{sec:ch3-architectures} and
Figure~\ref{fig:ch3-reasoner}; the training and checkpoint rules are given in
Section~\ref{sec:ch3-training}.

Three quantities are kept separate. Overall accuracy measures correct answers
across the complete 7,714-row view. A pooled four-or-more-step deficit describes
one system's relative accuracy profile across fixed-prior program-length
buckets. A paired deficit difference compares that profile between the
reasoner and fusion. The paired deficit difference is not an overall accuracy
gap, and it is taken directly from the canonical paired analysis rather than
reconstructed from displayed deficit levels.

Seed SD describes variation across trained seeds. Image-clustered intervals,
where available, describe evaluation-sampling uncertainty conditional on the
stored models, following Section~\ref{sec:ch3-statistics}. No reasoner
accuracy, overall accuracy gap, 100k or 250k deficit level, or token diagnostic
has an evaluation-sampling interval. Comparisons with the global fusion head
are configuration-level: architecture, token-level visual access and trainable
capacity change together, while seed counts and training streams are not fully
matched. The evidence cannot attribute a difference to any one of these
changes.

\section{Latent-reasoner scaling}
\label{sec:ch5-scaling}
The reasoner's stored seed means rise with the number of labelled training
questions. Mean development accuracy is 0.54222 at 40k, 0.57061 at 100k and
0.59580 at 250k. The corresponding seed SDs are 0.00366, 0.00020 and 0.00741.
Table~\ref{tab:ch5-scaling} gives the three constituent seed values as well as
the five-seed fusion reference. The 250k reasoner value of 0.60436 is one
seed-level observation; the three-seed mean of 0.59580 remains the reporting
unit.

The stored fusion means also increase, from 0.53840 to 0.56308 and 0.58226,
with seed SDs of 0.00218, 0.00268 and 0.00197. These series use the identical
7,714-question development denominator, but panel and table comparisons remain
descriptive because the reasoner has three seeds and fusion has five. As in the
global-head scaling study in Section~\ref{sec:ch4-scaling}, three training
sizes do not establish a general scaling law.

The seed-level rows explain why the mean and SD are reported together. The
three reasoner values are tightly grouped at 100k, whereas the 250k set contains
a higher seed-2 value and therefore a larger SD. This does not make the largest
seed the representative result. It shows that the stored mean at each scale
must be read alongside variation across its trained seeds. Fusion has a
separate five-seed distribution, so its smaller SD cannot be used as an
uncertainty estimate for the reasoner.

The ordered means provide evidence only for the three completed training
conditions. They do not supply an evaluation-sampling interval, a smooth
learning curve, or an estimate between the recorded scales. The comparison
therefore establishes that the stored reasoner improves across the three tested
training scales, while leaving extrapolation outside the measured settings
open.

\section{Reasoner against the global fusion head}
\label{sec:ch5-fusion}
The same-seed comparison uses seeds 0, 1 and 2 from both systems. At
40k, the canonical reasoner-minus-fusion accuracy gap is +0.00307 with seed SD
0.00622. Its seed-level values are +0.01024, -0.00025 and -0.00078, so two of
the three stored gaps are negative. This agrees with the bounded 40k finding
that the reasoner does not materially improve on fusion at this scale.

At 100k, the gap is +0.00652 with seed SD 0.00094; the three stored gaps are
+0.00713, +0.00544 and +0.00700. At 250k, it is +0.01361 with seed SD
0.00870; the corresponding values are +0.00700, +0.01037 and +0.02347.
Every stored seed therefore favours the reasoner at these two scales. There is
no image-clustered interval for any of these accuracy gaps, so this is
seed-consistent configuration-level evidence rather than an
evaluation-sampling claim.

Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-reasoner-fusion} places the accuracy series beside the
paired deficit contrasts, but the two panels show different quantities and
uncertainty. Panel (a) uses seed SD. Panel (b) uses image-clustered intervals
for reasoner deficit minus fusion deficit. The 40k value +0.00473 is therefore
not an accuracy gain: its positive sign means that the reasoner's pooled
deficit is larger. The corresponding 100k and 250k values are -0.00037 and
-0.00275. All three intervals include zero, so each paired deficit contrast is
not resolved clearly by its interval.

The reasoner has 21,099,620 trainable parameters, compared with 1,100,388 for
fusion, and it receives token-level rather than global visual features.
Architecture, access pattern and capacity all co-vary. The observed accuracy
advantage at 100k and 250k belongs to the implemented configuration; it does
not identify latent queries, token-level access or capacity as its cause.

\section{Token-level input diagnostics}
\label{sec:ch5-diagnostics}
The diagnostics test whether the reasoner is sensitive to the composition of
its token-level visual input. They do not identify an internal reasoning
mechanism. The D2 intervention removes the CLS token at test time from the
three trained 40k reasoners. Accuracy changes by -0.05017, -0.02839 and
-0.04161 for seeds 0, 1 and 2. Their pooled four-or-more-step lifts also fall,
from 0.15978 to 0.13810, from 0.16532 to 0.14718, and from 0.18246 to
0.15070. No interval was constructed for this intervention.

D3 asks a different question. It trains and evaluates a patches-only model
for seed 0, rather than altering a previously trained model at test time. Its
development accuracy is 0.53591, compared with 0.54641 for the full-token
seed-0 reasoner, and its pooled four-or-more-step lift is 0.14617 rather than
0.15978. Its shuffled-image drop is 0.1155, with 0.1101 reported for the
full-token seed-0 comparator. Because this is a single-seed training probe,
these values describe that configuration only.

Table~\ref{tab:ch5-diagnostics} makes the test-time and training-time
interventions explicit. The observational attention diagnostic supplies
context but not a mechanism: mean visual cross-attention mass on CLS was
0.3803, 0.3340 and 0.2642 across the three seeds, compared with a uniform token
share of 0.02. Attention mass does not explain a prediction or establish that
CLS contains a reasoning process. Together, the diagnostics support only that
the tested reasoner is sensitive to its token-input composition.

Two simpler reference heads also remain below the reasoner at 40k. Mean-patch
concatenation reaches 0.51449 with seed SD 0.00191, while a direct linear
classifier reaches 0.48125 with seed SD 0.00107. These reported references
place the diagnostic in context but do not isolate why the full reasoner
performs differently.

\section{Accuracy by reasoning depth}
\label{sec:ch5-depth}
Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-depth} groups the 40k development questions by GQA
semantic-program length. Program length is used as an in-distribution proxy for
reasoning depth; it is not a count of internal steps taken by a model. The
buckets contain 2,400 questions at two or fewer steps, 3,330 at three steps,
870 at four steps and 1,114 at five or more steps. The last two buckets pool to
1,984 questions for the four-or-more-step analysis.

Raw accuracy need not fall monotonically with program length because the
buckets have different answer priors. For example, at 40k the reasoner records
0.55944, 0.50791, 0.52605 and 0.62029 across the four buckets, while fusion
records 0.54942, 0.50312, 0.53425 and 0.62334. These values should not be read
as evidence that the longest questions are inherently easier, nor as a trace
of the models' internal computation.

The fixed-prior lift analysis adjusts each bucket against its stored question
prior before forming the pooled profile. The priors are 0.20917, 0.14625,
0.27356 and 0.51616; the pooled four-or-more-step prior is 0.40978. At 40k,
the combined four-or-more-step lifts are 0.16919 for the reasoner and 0.17449
for fusion, with image-clustered intervals [0.14046, 0.19768] and [0.14402,
0.20372]. This analysis describes relative difficulty under the fixed priors,
not compositional generalisation or internal reasoning depth.

The clustered intervals also follow the evaluation structure rather than the
training-seed structure. Questions attached to one image may share visual
content, so the represented image is the resampling unit. Each bucket has its
own image set: 674, 658, 396 and 531 images, with 620 images in the pooled
four-or-more-step view. The bucket intervals consequently describe sampling
uncertainty within those particular development slices; they are not
interchangeable with seed SD.

The pooled deficit combines two operations defined in
Section~\ref{sec:ch3-statistics}. First, each
bucket accuracy is compared with its fixed question prior to form a lift.
Second, the unweighted mean of all four lifts is compared with the
question-weighted combined lift for the four-step and five-or-more-step buckets.
This definition makes the deficit a profile statistic. It does not turn the
bucket order into a sequence of computations performed by the model.

\section{The pooled four-or-more-step deficit}
\label{sec:ch5-deficit}
Table~\ref{tab:ch5-deficits} reports deficit levels separately from paired
contrasts. At 40k, the reasoner level is 0.09795 with interval [0.08236,
0.11434], while the displayed five-seed fusion level is 0.09175 with interval
[0.07548, 0.10771]. At 100k and 250k the reasoner seed means are 0.08605 and
0.07753; the corresponding fusion means on seeds 0/1/2 are 0.08642 and
0.08028. No per-model interval exists for the 100k or 250k levels.

A lower pooled deficit should not be interpreted as higher overall accuracy or
stronger reasoning ability. It describes the relative difficulty profile under
the fixed step-depth analysis. This boundary is visible in the question-only
levels, which are 0.08090, 0.06897 and 0.06384 despite question only having
lower overall accuracy than the multimodal systems. Deficit ordering is not a
model-quality ranking.

The paired comparisons use canonical records on seeds 0/1/2. They are
reasoner deficit minus fusion deficit: +0.00473 [-0.00672, +0.01702] at 40k,
-0.00037 [-0.01211, +0.01057] at 100k, and -0.00275 [-0.01323, +0.00760]
at 250k. Positive means a larger reasoner deficit. Every interval includes
zero, so the paired deficit contrast is not resolved clearly at any scale.

The displayed 40k fusion level averages five seeds, while the paired 40k
contrast uses only seeds 0/1/2. The level cells must therefore not be
subtracted to reconstruct the paired value. The canonical paired records are
used directly at every scale, even where a displayed subtraction would happen
to give the recorded point estimate.

The reasoner level decreases across the three stored scale means, but that
within-system pattern answers a different question from the paired contrast.
The contrast asks whether the reasoner's profile differs from fusion's under
paired evaluation. Its point estimate changes sign after 40k, yet every
clustered interval spans zero. These intervals therefore do not resolve the
relative deficit profile clearly at any tested scale. In particular, the
positive and seed-consistent overall accuracy gaps at 100k and 250k cannot be
relabelled as evidence that fusion's multi-step profile has been improved upon.

\section{RQ2 summary}
\label{sec:ch5-summary}
On the 7,714-row top-100 development view, the 32-query latent reasoner rises
from 0.54222 to 0.57061 and 0.59580 mean accuracy as training increases from
40k to 100k and 250k questions. At 40k its canonical same-seed gap from fusion
is +0.00307 and is negative in two of three seeds. At 100k and 250k the gaps
are +0.00652 and +0.01361 and positive in every stored seed, although no
evaluation-sampling interval exists for these accuracy comparisons.

This is evidence about the implemented configuration. The reasoner changes
architecture, token-level visual access and trainable capacity together, and
the wider series use different seed counts and training streams. The token
diagnostics show sensitivity to input composition, but do not identify a
reasoning mechanism or explain the accuracy differences.

The fixed-prior analysis gives reasoner pooled four-or-more-step deficit levels
from 0.09795 to 0.07753 across the three scales. Its paired difference from
fusion has a zero-containing interval at every scale. Higher overall accuracy
therefore does not show that persistent multi-step difficulty has been removed.
Nor does the evidence establish that latent queries, token access or capacity
caused the gain; that either system performs compositional reasoning; that
program length measures internal reasoning; or that the three tested scales
define a scaling law. Broader mechanism and literature interpretation is
deferred to Chapter~8, and no held-out conclusion is made here.
