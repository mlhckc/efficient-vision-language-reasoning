\chapter{RQ3 — Frozen Small-Language-Model Interfaces}

\section{Scope and comparison logic}
\label{sec:ch6-scope}
RQ3 asks whether frozen small-language-model pretraining contributes differently
when it is placed on the question side or the answer side of the latent
reasoner. The interfaces are defined in Section~\ref{sec:ch3-architectures}
and Figure~\ref{fig:ch3-slm-interface}. All results use the top-100 development
view: 7,714 questions over 768 represented images, scored by G21 normalised
exact match. Every level and contrast uses seeds 0, 1 and 2 at 40k or 250k
training questions. The chapter reports development evidence only and makes no
held-out claim.

Every first-order pretraining contrast is the pretrained arm minus its
architecture-matched random control at the corresponding model size; positive
values favour pretraining. The 360M difference in differences instead
subtracts the 40k first-order effect from the 250k first-order effect. It is a
second-order interaction across training scales, not a simple
pretrained-minus-random effect. Seed SD describes variation across trained
seeds. Contrast intervals describe image-clustered evaluation-sampling
uncertainty conditional on those seeds, following
Section~\ref{sec:ch3-statistics}.

The stored directional rule is applied to the E8A, E8B and E10 contrasts in
this chapter. Positive directional evidence requires a positive mean effect,
an interval whose lower bound is above zero, and positive effects in at least
two of the three seeds. Negative directional evidence uses the mirrored
criteria. All other governed cases are described as uncertain or mixed
evidence. The intervals are nominal and unadjusted, so their role is to bound
each reported contrast; an interval containing zero does not resolve it.

Checkpoint selection bounds interpretation. The question-side E8A arms select the best development checkpoint. Answer-side B2, B3, B4r and B4 use the
protocol-fixed epoch-22 checkpoint. Their within-family pretraining contrasts
are selection-matched, but cross-family readings are juxtapositions rather than
a factorial placement experiment. The LM-free B1 reference uses
best-development selection and is contextual beside the fixed-epoch arms.
Only the matched first-order pairs answer the narrow pretraining question.
A1 versus A0p changes representation and interface, while comparisons with B1
also change checkpoint-selection class. The chapter uses only the two recorded
training scales and does not interpolate between them.

\section{Question-side interface: pretrained, random and CLIP reference}
\label{sec:ch6-question}
A0p projects frozen CLIP question tokens into the reasoner and is the CLIP-token
reference. A1 uses frozen pretrained SmolLM2-135M hidden states, while A1r uses
architecture-matched frozen random weights with the identical tokenizer and
interface. A1 and A1r each have 21,395,044 trainable parameters; A0p has
21,362,276. These counts establish near matching and capacity context only.
Within the A1--A1r pair, frozen weight initialisation is the intended change;
tokenizer, hidden width, projection and trainable parameter count are held
fixed. A0p instead supplies CLIP-aligned question tokens through a different
projection input, which is why it provides a useful reference but not the
architecture-matched pretraining control.

Table~\ref{tab:ch6-question-arms} reports the stored development levels. At
40k, A0p reaches $0.54045\pm0.00438$, A1 reaches
$0.52861\pm0.00296$, and A1r reaches $0.49041\pm0.00259$.
At 250k the corresponding values are $0.59446\pm0.00030$,
$0.57316\pm0.00154$, and $0.52480\pm0.00487$. These uncertainties are seed
SDs, not clustered intervals. Mean accuracy is higher at 250k than at 40k for
every reported arm, but these scale changes are not ranked against the
pretraining effects.

The clean within-interface comparison is A1 minus A1r. At 40k, the effect is
$+0.03820$. Its interval is $[0.02947,0.04680]$. At 250k, the effect is
$+0.04835$, with interval $[0.03935,0.05803]$. Its paired seed effects are positive at both
scales, giving positive directional evidence. SmolLM2 pretraining therefore
helps relative to its matched random control within this question-side
interface. More specifically, the paired effects are $+0.03565$, $+0.04304$
and $+0.03591$ at 40k, then $+0.04472$, $+0.04874$ and $+0.05159$ at
250k. The agreement across seeds complements the intervals; it does not turn
this implemented comparison into a general claim about pretrained language
models.

A1 minus A0p is $-0.01184$ with interval $[-0.01996,-0.00427]$ at 40k and
$-0.02130$ with interval $[-0.02983,-0.01253]$ at 250k, giving negative
directional evidence at both scales. This comparison is secondary and
\texttt{REPRESENTATION\_CONFOUNDED}: representation and interface change
together, and A0p is not A1's random control. The bounded result is that
pretraining helps within the SmolLM2 interface, while the pretrained interface
remains below the CLIP-token reference under the tested configurations. It
does not establish intrinsic representation quality. All three paired
A1-minus-A0p effects are negative at both scales. This supports the stored
negative directional classification while retaining the comparison's
secondary status and its representation confounding.

\section{Answer-side interface at 135M}
\label{sec:ch6-answer135}
The answer-side language model receives only a BOS embedding followed by 32
projected latent vectors through \texttt{inputs\_embeds}; raw question text
never enters it. The governed R1 readout scores every candidate in the top-100
answer vocabulary by likelihood. It is not free-form answer generation. B2
uses a random frozen SmolLM2-135M readout and B3 uses the architecture-matched
pretrained readout. Both have 21,343,808 trainable parameters and use fixed
epoch 22. This interface tests pretraining after the question and image have
already been compressed into the reasoner's latent sequence. It therefore
differs substantively from the question-side interface, where frozen
language-model states supply the question representation before latent
reasoning. The cross-placement comparison concerns these complete implemented
configurations; it does not isolate where any difference originates.

At 40k, B2 records $0.53392\pm0.00233$, whereas B3 records
$0.50929\pm0.02795$. The pretrained arm has high dispersion: its accuracies
are 0.53124, 0.47783 and 0.51880. B3 minus B2 is $-0.02463$ with interval
$[-0.03333,-0.01619]$; all paired effects are negative, so this is negative
directional evidence. At 250k, B2 reaches $0.60086\pm0.00528$ and B3 reaches
$0.59610\pm0.00270$. The first-order contrast is $-0.00475$ with interval
$[-0.01208,+0.00297]$, and its seed signs disagree. Its governed outcome is
uncertain or mixed evidence. The 40k paired effects are $-0.00091$,
$-0.05872$ and $-0.01426$, so all three support the negative direction
despite the large variation in B3's arm levels. At 250k the effects are
$+0.00233$, $-0.00324$ and $-0.01335$. Their disagreement and the
zero-containing interval prevent the negative point estimate from being
reported as negative directional evidence.

B1 is an LM-free attention-pool reference with 21,100,132 trainable parameters.
It records $0.54507\pm0.00390$ at 40k and $0.59394\pm0.00486$ at 250k. B1 is
selected by best development accuracy, whereas B2 and B3 use fixed epoch 22.
The B1 levels in Table~\ref{tab:ch6-answer-arms} are descriptive context, not
the primary pretraining control or a selection-matched attribution basis. In
particular, neither B2-minus-B1 nor B3-minus-B1 answers whether pretrained
weights help within the frozen language-model readout. That question is
carried by B3 minus B2, for which both arms share the fixed-epoch rule and the
shared 135M architecture.

Figure~\ref{fig:ch6-pretraining} combines the controlled first-order contrasts
and the second-order interaction. The sign of a point estimate is insufficient;
the governed outcome also uses its clustered interval and paired seed signs.

\section{Answer-side capacity: the 360M pair}
\label{sec:ch6-answer360}
The 360M pair retains the answer-side interface while changing the frozen
language-model size. B4r is random and B4 is pretrained; each configuration has
21,540,800 trainable parameters. The nominal 360M frozen-model size is distinct
from this approximately 21.5M trainable component. Thus the B4--B4r pair is
internally matched in architecture and trainable capacity, whereas comparing
it with the 135M pair changes frozen model size as well as experiment family.
The 360M results test whether a positive pretraining contribution appears
within that larger matched pair, not whether 360M is intrinsically preferable
to 135M.

At 40k, B4r records $0.53319\pm0.00473$ and B4 records
$0.52636\pm0.00466$. B4 minus B4r is $-0.00683$ with interval
$[-0.01508,+0.00114]$. At 250k the levels are $0.59645\pm0.00343$ and
$0.59407\pm0.00831$, while the contrast is $-0.00238$ with interval
$[-0.01071,+0.00588]$. Seed signs disagree at both scales, and both governed
outcomes are uncertain or mixed evidence. Neither result supports a positive
answer-side pretraining advantage; both remain unresolved by their intervals.
The paired effects are $-0.00959$, $+0.00363$ and $-0.01452$ at 40k, and
$+0.00959$, $-0.01219$ and $-0.00454$ at 250k. In both cases the seed signs
disagree. Mean accuracy is nevertheless higher at 250k for both B4r and B4;
that scale pattern is separate from the within-scale pretraining contrasts.

The difference in differences is
$(\mathrm{B4}-\mathrm{B4r})_{250k}-(\mathrm{B4}-\mathrm{B4r})_{40k}$.
It is $+0.00445$ with interval $[-0.00709,+0.01641]$, with paired seed effects
$+0.01919$, $-0.01582$ and $+0.00998$. A positive sign means the first-order
pretraining effect is more positive, or less negative, at 250k than at 40k.
The interval includes zero and seed directions disagree, so the governed result
is uncertain or mixed evidence. This second-order interaction does not show
that pretraining improves with more data, that capacity benefits pretraining,
or that the 360M model helps.

\section{Placement synthesis}
\label{sec:ch6-placement}
Table~\ref{tab:ch6-contrasts} makes the contrast order explicit. Six rows
compare pretrained and random weights inside a matched interface and model
size. The seventh compares two first-order effects across scale. Separating
these quantities prevents the positive DiD estimate from being mistaken for a
positive pretraining effect. The table also prevents comparisons based only on
arm means. Each first-order effect is paired within seed and evaluated with its
stored clustered interval, whereas the arm tables report seed SDs. The two
uncertainty summaries answer different questions and are not interchangeable.
For the DiD, the reported per-seed effects belong to the second-order contrast
itself rather than to either single-scale pretraining comparison.

The evidence differs by placement. On the question side, pretrained
SmolLM2-135M has positive directional evidence over its matched random control
at both scales, yet remains below the CLIP-token reference in a secondary,
representation-confounded comparison. On the 135M answer side, the first-order
contrast is negative directional at 40k and uncertain or mixed at 250k. At
360M, both first-order contrasts and the scale interaction are uncertain or
mixed.

The governed directions are asymmetric. Question-side pretraining has positive
evidence against its random control at both scales. Answer-side pretraining has
negative evidence only for the 135M pair at 40k. The other three answer-side
first-order cases are not resolved as directional, and the uncertain 360M DiD
does not change their classifications.

This supports an interface-specific conclusion under the implemented
configurations. It does not establish a general property of frozen language
models, because question-side, answer-side and capacity experiments were run
separately and their selection rules differ across families. It also does not
identify why the placements differ. Explanations involving representation
alignment, frozen language space or internal mechanism are deferred to
Chapter~8.

\section{RQ3 accuracy summary}
\label{sec:ch6-summary}
On the common 7,714-question top-100 development view, frozen SmolLM2-135M
pretraining helps relative to its matched random control on the question side:
A1 minus A1r is positive directional at 40k and 250k. The pretrained interface
remains below the CLIP-token A0p reference under the tested configurations,
with negative directional A1-minus-A0p contrasts at both scales. This secondary
comparison is representation-confounded.

The answer-side results do not show a reliable positive pretraining advantage.
B3 minus B2 gives negative directional evidence at 40k and uncertain or mixed
evidence at 250k. B4 minus B4r is uncertain or mixed at both scales, as is the
second-order interaction between its pretraining effect and training scale.
These findings leave the zero-containing contrasts unresolved rather than
showing that pretrained and random weights are interchangeable.

Under the implemented interfaces, the benefit of frozen SmolLM2 pretraining is
interface-specific rather than universal. This is a bounded cross-experiment
synthesis of three separately run experiments, not a factorial causal
placement study. It does not show that question-side pretraining is the
strongest representation, that larger capacity resolves the answer-side
pattern, or that the result generalises beyond these development configurations.
Efficiency evidence for these interfaces is analysed separately in Chapter 7.
